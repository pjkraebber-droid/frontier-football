## 📋 TOP STORY

**Vermont Green FC are back-to-back USL League Two champions — and they're the first club in USL2 history to do it.**


Vermont Green FC won their second consecutive USL League Two title Saturday night in Flint, Michigan, taking down the Flint City Bucks 2–1. Ryan Zellefrow's 76th-minute penalty-kick goal in front of 6,396 fans at Atwood Stadium sealed the result, making the Green the first back-to-back champion in League Two history and only the fourth club ever to repeat in the broader league lineage.


The game itself took until the second half to catch fire. 
Vermont struck first in the 62nd minute when a Diego Rosas cross was deflected into the net by a Flint City player, but Bucks midfielder Matthew Paiva answered immediately on a header to level it at 1–1 just two minutes later.
 The decisive moment came from a late substitute's hustle: 
David Ajagbe, on since the 73rd minute, drew a penalty after being brought down in the box by Flint City goalkeeper Steve Clark. Zellefrow stepped up and slotted home the winner, earning Final MVP honors.



Zellefrow scored five goals and assisted three others across the team's five-game playoff run.
 
The win also cements Vermont's second straight undefeated season, with the club winning its second Northeast Division title and Eastern Conference championship en route to the Final.
 
Vermont finishes the 2026 campaign with a record of 18 wins and one draw.


Flint was no pushover. 
The Bucks had won their fourth consecutive Great Lakes Division title and had eliminated FC Buffalo and Steel City FC in the Central Conference bracket before a dominant 4–0 takedown of Peoria City in the conference final.
 
Their national semifinal against Ventura County Fusion was scoreless into stoppage time before Oliver Moller-Jensen converted late to send Flint through to its seventh League Two Final.
 Vermont had the better of the season's narrative, but Flint's run was legitimate.

This is a program that should be taken seriously at the pyramid level. Burlington, Vermont is not a typical soccer market, and the club has built something structurally unusual. Worth watching what comes next.
